Your legs, your feet, your balance they all depend on those nerves having room.

The multifidus is the muscle that holds that tunnel open.

When it’s working, the tunnel stays open. The nerves have space. Your legs work.

When it switches off, the tunnel closes. The nerves get squashed.

That’s when the burning starts. The numbness. The heavy legs. The feeling like you’re wading through treacle.

And here’s the part your doctor never explained:

Every time you stand up, your body weight presses down on your spine.

When the muscle is dead, there’s nothing pushing back. Your weight squashes the tunnel shut.

That’s why standing in the queue hurts more than walking.
